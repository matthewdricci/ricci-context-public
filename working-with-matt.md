## Clarity is kindness

Unclear feedback delivered warmly still leaves people confused. Clear feedback delivered harshly still moves things forward.

|             | ✅ Clear | ❌ Unclear |
|-------------|:--------:|:----------:|
| **Warm**    | 😊😊😊   | 😞         |
| **Harsh**   | 😊       | 😞😞😞     |

If you have to choose between being clear or being warm, choose clear. But the best communicators are both. Taken from Brené Brown, who has the better quote, but is too long for my daily use: "Clear is Kind. Unclear is Unkind."

## Default to Written Communication

- Written communication should be the default for most work. It forces clarity of thought, creates a record for others to reference, and respects everyone's time.
- Avoid using calls or meetings as a shortcut for thinking through problems. Prepare your thoughts in writing; only escalate to synchronous communication when necessary for true discussion, brainstorming, or when tone and nuance are critical.

## Slack and Chat Etiquette

- **Don't Say "Hello" and Wait:** In chat, avoid sending messages that just say "hi" or "hello" and waiting for a response. This creates unnecessary delays. Instead, greet and state your question or request in the same message.

- **Efficient Messaging:** Combine pleasantries and your actual inquiry into one message. This allows for asynchronous communication—if the recipient is away, they can respond to your question directly when they return.

- **Public Channels over DMs:** Default to public channels for most work communication to promote transparency, reduce redundant questions, and avoid notification overload.

- **Use Threads and Emoji:** Use threads to keep discussions organized and minimize noise in channels. Emoji reactions can convey approval, agreement, or status updates quickly without cluttering conversations.

- **Set Expectations:** Use channel descriptions and statuses to clarify response times, especially for distributed teams. Leverage Do Not Disturb modes to protect focus and off-hours.
